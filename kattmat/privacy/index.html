Privacy policy

Kattmat for iPhone · Last updated 16 November 2026

Hi, I’m Ruby. Your household lives on your iPhone, and we never see it. No account. No ads. No tracking.

The short version

  • What you put in Kattmat stays on your iPhone. Kattmat has no server. We can’t see your household.
  • We collect nothing. No analytics, no ads, no tracking.
  • Your household goes to iCloud only if you turn on “Keep in iCloud” or share it. Then it goes only to people you invite.
  • Apple may send us summaries without your name. Apart from that, we only hear from you if you email us or test a beta.

What Kattmat stores

Kattmat stores only what you put in:

  • your pets: name, kind of animal, birthday, looks, care guide, next vet visit, and vet and emergency contacts;
  • for a pet who has passed away: the day, and how they’re remembered;
  • routines, and who marked them done and when;
  • weigh-ins, and notes with who wrote them;
  • your name and phone number, if you type them into a sitter checklist;
  • a sitter’s name, from a checklist you import;
  • if you share: the people in your household, and a sitter’s days.

It also keeps its settings and your home’s time zone. It keeps what it knows about your plan. And it keeps who and which spaces your sharing has. All of this stays on your iPhone. None of it is sent to us.

iCloud

With “Keep in iCloud” on, your household is also kept in your own private iCloud. Only your Apple Account can read it until you share it. Kattmat keeps a copy on your iPhone of what it sent. That way it knows what changed.

Kattmat needs to know which person in the household is you. So it saves the identifier iCloud gives each person’s Apple Account. This is not an email address or a name. The identifiers stay after someone leaves, and after iCloud is turned off. Backup files leave them out.

Each time you open Kattmat, it checks with iCloud whether your household is shared and with whom. It does this even when “Keep in iCloud” is off. It sends nothing from your household when it checks. When something changes, Apple’s notification service tells your devices. Apple’s privacy policy covers iCloud.

Sharing your household

People you invite get your household through Apple’s iCloud sharing. The invite shows the owner’s name and the pets. A sitter’s invite also shows their name and days. We never see a shared household.

A partner

A partner shares the whole household with you. They see everything and can change it, just like you. Once they’ve joined, your plan is kept in the shared household too. Their Kattmat reads it to know what it may do. It holds:

  • which plan you have, and whether it’s a trial;
  • when it ends, and whether it renews;
  • a failed payment, and how long Apple keeps the plan going;
  • a refund;
  • the last day Apple confirmed the plan.

This stays in your iCloud until you delete the household from iCloud.

A sitter

A sitter gets a space of their own. It holds only what they need:

  • the pets they look after: name, kind of animal, birthday, looks, care guide, next vet visit and contacts;
  • the routines;
  • what was marked done and written on their days;
  • your name and your partner’s name.

A sitter doesn’t get your older History or weigh-ins. They don’t see pets in memory or your plan. They don’t see other sitters’ notes or anyone’s Apple Account.

What a sitter does reaches your household only through your iPhone or your partner’s, with their name on it. Apple’s sharing shows a sitter the names of the people their space is shared with. It also shows an identifier Apple keeps for each account. Kattmat can’t hide those.

A sitter’s access ends after their last day. You or your partner can end it sooner, and the sitter can leave by themselves in Kattmat. Kattmat then removes the household from their iPhone. If their iPhone can’t reach iCloud, the household stays hidden until it can.

Your iPhone then closes the sitter’s space in iCloud. It waits a day after the last day, so that anything marked done on an offline phone still arrives. If you end the access early, it waits an hour. It closes the space the next time Kattmat runs after that.

A sitter checklist

The checklist PDF holds your pets, the care guide, the contacts and the routines. It also holds the sitter’s name. Your name and phone number are on it if you add them. It goes only where you and your sitter send it. Kattmat reads the returned copy on your iPhone.

What we collect

Nothing. Kattmat has no analytics and no crash-reporting service. It has no ads and no tracking. It doesn’t ask to track you.

Your iPhone’s settings let you share crash reports or usage data with app developers. If you do, Apple collects them. Apple then shows us summaries without your name. This is covered by Apple’s privacy policy.

Reminders are planned on your iPhone. That includes the one before your trial ends. Kattmat has no server of its own that sends them. Siri, Shortcuts and widgets also run on your iPhone. Apple’s privacy policy covers what Siri does with your voice.

This page and the support page are hosted on GitHub Pages. Opening them is a visit to GitHub, also when you open them from the app. GitHub’s privacy statement covers that.

Backups

When you back up your household, you choose where the file goes. Files or Mail, for example. It stays there until you delete it.

Kattmat also keeps automatic backups on your iPhone. It makes one before a big change, such as a restore, an invite or joining a household. These backups include the people in the household and their identifiers.

Kattmat also keeps a copy of its data when an update changes how it stores it. It does the same if it can’t open its data. It keeps the newest three of each kind.

Purchases

You buy plans and treats for Ruby through the App Store. Apple handles the payment. We never see your payment details.

From the App Store, Kattmat learns:

  • which plan you have, and whether it’s a trial;
  • when it ends, and whether it renews;
  • whether a payment failed or was refunded;
  • whether you can still get the free trial.

It keeps this on your iPhone. Once a partner has joined, it also keeps it in your iCloud.

Writing to us

If you email us, we get your email address and the content of your email. Your email stays in our iCloud Mail, which is Apple’s email service. We use it only to answer you. We delete it once your question is settled, within 12 months at the latest. We never share it.

Testing a beta

You can test Kattmat through Apple’s TestFlight. Then Apple shares with us:

  • the name and email address we invited you with (nothing if you joined through a public link);
  • your device model and iOS version;
  • how often you open the beta;
  • crash reports;
  • any feedback and screenshots you send.

We use them only to fix Kattmat. We never share them. We delete what we hold within 30 days after the beta ends, or after you ask. That means you as a tester, your feedback and screenshots, and your emails to us.

Apple keeps its own copy for a time under its TestFlight privacy terms. We can’t delete it. You can manage it at privacy.apple.com.

Deleting your data

Delete Kattmat, and everything it stored on your iPhone goes with it. Backup files you saved stay until you delete them.

When a sitter’s access ends, Kattmat removes your household from their iPhone. A thank-you note stays until they close it. It has your name, your pets’ names, their name and their last day.

When a partner leaves or you remove them, Kattmat takes your household off their iPhone. It also removes the automatic backups made while they were in it. A copy of its data from an app update in that time can stay on their iPhone.

What sitters and partners marked done and wrote stays in your History.

To remove your household from iCloud as well:

  1. If a sitter still has access, end that first. In Settings, under People, tap “End …’s access…”
  2. Wait an hour. Then open Kattmat again with “Keep in iCloud” still on. Kattmat closes the sitter’s space.
  3. On the owner’s iPhone, with “Keep in iCloud” on, open Settings. Tap “Delete household from iCloud…”

Everything stays on your iPhone. Sharing with your partner ends.

Children

Kattmat is fine for all ages. It collects nothing from anyone, children included.

Your rights

Diliara Tazieva is responsible for the personal data in this policy. Under the GDPR, that makes her the “controller”. The data is only an email you send us. If you test a beta, it is also what TestFlight shares with us.

  • Why we may use it: to answer you and to fix Kattmat. That is our legitimate interest (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Who else handles it: Apple keeps our email (iCloud Mail) and runs TestFlight. GitHub hosts these pages. Both may store data in the US. Apple does so under the EU’s standard contractual clauses. GitHub does so under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.
  • How long: We delete an email conversation once your question is settled. At the latest, we delete it 12 months after the last message. For beta data, see Testing a beta.

You can ask to see what we hold about you. You can ask us to correct it, delete it or limit how we use it. You can also object to our using it at any time. Email kattmat.app@icloud.com. We usually answer within a few days, and always within a month.

Do you think we handle your data wrongly? Then you can file a complaint with the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY), or with the data protection authority where you live or work.
